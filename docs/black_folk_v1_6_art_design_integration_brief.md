# BLACK FOLK V1.6 — Art & Design Integration Brief

## Goal
Deepen BLACK FOLK's CULTURE world without bloating the 50-node core.

The expansion uses three complementary sources:
- Richard J. Powell, *Black Art and Culture in the 20th Century*
- *The Black Experience in Design: Identity, Expression & Reflection*
- *Black Futures*, edited by Kimberly Drew and Jenna Wortham

## Locked structural decision
- Core nodes remain **50**.
- Worlds remain **7**.
- Existing debates remain **8**.
- Formal threads become **10**.
- Deepening cards become **14**.

## New formal thread
### WHO DESIGNS THE WORLD?
**Question:** Who shapes the images, spaces, systems, platforms, and futures Black people inhabit—and who has the power to redesign them?

This is deliberately broader than “art history.” It lets visual art, literature, music, photography, film, design, media, public space, services, technology, and cultural institutions live in one connected intellectual path.

## New deepening cards
1. **Who Controls the Image?** — representation, stereotype, self-definition.
2. **Who Is Black Art For?** — audience, patronage, autonomy, political obligation.
3. **Art as Evidence** — photography, witness, circulation, EVIDENCE AS RESISTANCE.
4. **The Artist as Organizer** — collectives, guilds, community art spaces, AfriCOBRA.
5. **Systems Are Designed Too** — institutions and services as designed arrangements.
6. **Design as Care** — healing, accessibility, participation, power-sharing.
7. **Who Owns the Platform?** — Black-controlled media, digital visibility, surveillance, co-option.
8. **Who Gets to Imagine the Future?** — futurity, Black imagination, speculative design.

## Why each source is here
### Powell
Supplies the historical visual-art spine:
- stereotype vs counter-image
- New Negro / Harlem Renaissance debates
- audience and patronage
- art and labor
- photojournalism and civil-rights witness
- Black Power aesthetics
- artist collectives / AfriCOBRA
- confrontation with the Western art canon

### The Black Experience in Design
Extends “art” into designed systems and institutions:
- services, transport, care, spaces, interfaces
- Black-centered design communities
- solidarity and support structures
- community design and healing
- identity and self-definition
- speculative futures

### Black Futures
Supplies the contemporary cultural/archive layer:
- Black life as an archival project
- mixed media as knowledge
- visibility vs surveillance/co-option
- digital platforms
- preserving Black life for future generations
- imagining Black futures

## Hidden-lens fit
**What Now?** asks what Black people should build, own, control, and sustain.

**The End of Running** asks what structures reproduce the problem and how organized people change them.

This art/design layer adds a third recurring question:

> **WHO DESIGNED THIS — AND WHO COULD REDESIGN IT?**

It keeps art connected to ownership, institutions, power, organizing, collective capacity, and imagination.

## Product guardrail
Do not turn this into an art-history survey.

No artist-name trivia.
No medium checklist.
No giant new branch.

A piece enters BLACK FOLK only when it teaches an idea that connects to the existing network.
