## New core nodes

### V1-045 — Respectability
**World:** IDENTITY  
**Core idea:** Respectability is the strategy or expectation that Black people can gain legitimacy, protection, opportunity, or acceptance by meeting dominant standards of behavior, appearance, morality, class, or public presentation.  
**KEEP THIS:** If protection depends on being respectable, are they really rights?  
**Threads:** T-09  
**Connections:** V1-018, V1-021, V1-023, V1-033, V1-039, V1-040, V1-049, V1-050  
**Encounter seeds:** 4

### V1-046 — Abolition
**World:** FREEDOM  
**Core idea:** Abolition is a Black intellectual and political tradition that asks whether some systems must be ended rather than repaired—and what social institutions must exist so that the ending produces a durable form of freedom.  
**KEEP THIS:** Ending something is only half the question. What replaces it?  
**Threads:** T-01  
**Connections:** V1-014, V1-015, V1-020, V1-049, CTX-05  
**Encounter seeds:** 4

### V1-047 — Black Nationalism
**World:** POWER  
**Core idea:** Black nationalism is a varied tradition of Black self-determination. Different nationalists have emphasized territory, political unity, economic control, culture, independent institutions, or combinations of these.  
**KEEP THIS:** Representation asks who is present. Self-determination asks who can decide.  
**Threads:** T-01, T-08  
**Connections:** V1-012, V1-030, V1-036, V1-037, V1-048  
**Encounter seeds:** 4

### V1-048 — Pan-Africanism
**World:** ORGANIZING  
**Core idea:** Pan-Africanism is a family of political and cultural projects that tries to turn connections among African-descended peoples into deliberate solidarity, organization, or cooperation across national borders.  
**KEEP THIS:** Diaspora describes connection. Pan-Africanism tries to organize it.  
**Threads:** T-08  
**Connections:** V1-001, V1-036, V1-037, V1-042, V1-047  
**Encounter seeds:** 4

### V1-049 — Criminalization
**World:** POWER  
**Core idea:** Criminalization is the process through which laws, institutions, media, and social narratives define particular people, behaviors, or communities as dangerous and therefore more legitimately punishable or controllable.  
**KEEP THIS:** Before punishment comes a decision about who looks punishable.  
**Threads:** T-03, T-09  
**Connections:** V1-002, V1-021, V1-039, V1-045, V1-046, CTX-02, CTX-04, CTX-05  
**Encounter seeds:** 4

### V1-050 — Predatory Inclusion
**World:** ECONOMICS  
**Core idea:** Predatory inclusion describes access granted to people previously excluded from a market or institution, but on unequal or exploitative terms that can reproduce extraction rather than equal power.  
**KEEP THIS:** Getting in matters. So do the terms of entry.  
**Threads:** T-07  
**Connections:** V1-017, V1-032, V1-045, V1-047, T-07, CTX-04  
**Encounter seeds:** 4


## Formal thread map

1. **LEAVE · REFORM · BUILD** — Do you leave an oppressive institution, reform it, or build another one?
2. **LEVERAGE** — How do people without formal authority make powerful institutions respond?
3. **EVIDENCE AS RESISTANCE** — How can research challenge ideas presented as objective truth?
4. **WHAT IS EDUCATION FOR?** — Is education for literacy, livelihood, leadership, institutions, political consciousness—or several at once?
5. **WHO BUILT IT?** — What invisible infrastructure sits underneath the famous event or leader?
6. **WHO DEFINES BLACKNESS?** — Who gets to define what Black life means?
7. **LEGAL VICTORY · LIVED REALITY** — What changes when the law changes—and what does not?
8. **BLACKNESS ACROSS BORDERS** — What connects Black people across borders, and where does the connection break?
9. **RESPECTABILITY ↔ REFUSAL** — Should you meet their standards or refuse them, and who pays for each choice?

## Recurring question

> **WHO IS “WE”?**

Use it when Black politics speaks collectively. Ask whose experience, interests, or identity is being treated as representative of the group.

This is intentionally **not** a formal thread. It should feel like a reflex the player develops.

## Key integration rules

- Keep **V1-002 Law helped construct race** focused on racial category-making.
- Route Black Codes, vagrancy, danger, and punishability to **V1-049 Criminalization**.
- **Abolition** is a real named intellectual tradition, but it always sits inside a neutral **FIX IT OR END IT?** debate and the broader **LEAVE · REFORM · BUILD** thread.
- **Predatory Inclusion** does not replace **LEGAL VICTORY · LIVED REALITY**. The thread names the pattern; Predatory Inclusion names one mechanism.
- **Respectability ↔ Refusal** must never imply that refusal is automatically superior. Present why serious Black organizers used respectability strategically.
- **BLACKNESS ACROSS BORDERS** must show a connection being made or broken. Geography alone does not earn a card.
- No new WORDS quotations were added for the six nodes. Quote QA should remain separate.

## Black women / Black feminist integration

Do not treat Black women only as famous-person biography. The V1.5 structure should continue to use:
- Maria Stewart → who is included in Black freedom
- Ida B. Wells → evidence, leverage, self-protection
- Mary Church Terrell → education/status converted into public action
- Callie House → mass grassroots organization
- Mary McLeod Bethune → independent institution-building + access to state power
- Ella Baker → participatory leadership
- Combahee → Black feminist analysis, coalition, and **WHO IS “WE”?**
- Anita Hill → race/gender and conflicting political loyalties
- **Respectability ↔ Refusal** → ordinary, poor, criminalized, and non-respectable Black women as sources of political thought
- Womanist theology, Anna Julia Cooper, Black queer archive work → strong deepening/future material, not automatic core-node additions
